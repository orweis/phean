# Design System: Phean

The colour tokens above are the default theme, Dusk; the `dark-` entries are the same tokens in its dark appearance. Every theme's values live in `src/lib/theme-tokens.ts`, the source of truth, and `pnpm themes` writes them into `src/app/themes.css`, which `src/app/globals.css` imports for everything else (type, radii, motion, the utilities). The generated `@theme` block resets Tailwind's default palette (`--color-*: initial`), so a class like `text-zinc-500` does not exist here: every colour is one of these names, and components never name a theme or a dark colour themselves.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Decision Center"**

Agents do the tasks; people make the calls. The app is where those calls are made, so it stays quiet and exact, and the site is where the idea is sold, so it is bright and moving. The logo keeps the boulder on its slope. The marketing site explains the name once, in a drawing of agents rolling the boulder up the hill. The ledger itself is quiet. A quiet tinted ground holds lighter cards ruled in hairlines, and the only material that floats is a Liquid Glass bar on top, in Apple's manner: the functional layer of navigation and menus sits on glass above the content and lets the content show through, blurred, as it scrolls beneath.

The density is that of an operations screen, not a marketing page. People check in a few times a day to see what agents did, what is late, and what awaits their decision or answer, so the overview leads with the line, the agents' questions and the decisions, and every figure is a tabular numeral with its label. Colour carries meaning only: the brand (`accent`) is primary actions and work in motion, the signal (the boulder's ember) is what is new and what needs a person, and each state has one hue on every screen, in every theme.

Each person picks a colour theme (Dusk, the default; Ledger, Phean's first look; Ink; or Console) and, separately, Light, Dark, or System (the default, which follows the device and changes with it), in the account menu, on the Account page, or in the site's footer before signing in. Every theme has both appearances: in dark the ground turns to the theme's night and the glass smokes with it, and the hues lift to keep their contrast.

**Key Characteristics:**

- A sticky Liquid Glass header with the boulder wordmark, a workspace switcher and a capsule tab rail; content scrolls under it and fades at its edge.
- One brand hue (`accent`: Dusk's periwinkle, Ledger's violet, Ink's teal, Console's blue) for primary actions, the selected tab, focus and work in progress; one warm signal for the boulder, the counts on the rail and the call to action on a dusk band.
- Capsule controls throughout (buttons, tabs, badges, segmented controls); cards keep a 14px continuous-looking corner.
- SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter Variable with optical sizing everywhere else; SF Pro Rounded for figures where it exists.
- State is carried by line form as well as hue: dashed while waiting, solid while moving, a dot on arrival, a slash when dropped.
- Four themes, each with Light and Dark (and System), every text pair at 4.5:1 or better and every line and control at 3:1 or better in all eight palettes, checked by `tests/themes.test.ts`.

## Colors

A tinted ground under one brand hue, with one warm signal and four state hues that mean the same thing on every screen. The roles below are the same in every theme; the values are Dusk's, the default (light · dark). Names such as "ember" and "violet" in the rest of this file name a role (the signal, the brand), whatever hue a theme gives it.

### Primary

- **Brand** (`accent`, Dusk's periwinkle, oklch(45% 0.13 274) · dark oklch(77% 0.1 274)): the primary button, the active tab's capsule (as `accent-tint` with `accent-ink`), the selected workspace's check, the hero's second sentence, focus rings (`focus`), and the in-progress station. `accent-2` is its hover. Text on it is `on-accent`: white in Dusk's light, Dusk's dark slate on its light periwinkle in dark (7.6:1 and 8.9:1); brand text on the ground is `accent-ink`.
- **The hill** (`hill`): the slope in the logo. It is the accent in light and a lighter cut in dark and on any dusk band (`dusk-hill`), so the mark holds on a dark ground.

### Secondary

- **Signal** (`signal`, Dusk's apricot, oklch(79% 0.13 60) · dark oklch(81% 0.11 62)): the boulder in the logo, the tab rail's counts of what needs a person, everything waiting on a person (as `wait`), the travelling requests in the marketing site's decisions band, and the one call to action on a dusk band. Labels on it are `on-signal`, always dark. `signal-shade` is the boulder's shaded side; `signal-ink` is signal text, used only in the accent badge on `signal-tint`. Every theme keeps it warm (an ember, apricot or amber, hue 40 to 90), never red.

### Tertiary

- **Late red** (`late`, `late-ink`, `late-tint`): the now line, late tracks and cards, urgent priority, and destructive buttons. Red in every theme (hue 15 to 30).
- **Waiting** (`wait`, `wait-ink`, `wait-tint`): what waits on a person, in the theme's own signal (goals awaiting approval, and agent questions: the Waiting on you badge, the question under it on a card, the rule beside it on the overview), high priority, read-only access. `wait` and `wait-tint` are the signal itself, by reference; `wait-ink` is a deeper cut for text on its tint.
- **Inbox** (`inbox`, `inbox-ink`, `inbox-tint`): what just arrived and is still to triage: the inbox station, its line and its badge. A cool sky (Dusk's is a misty blue, Console's ice), so it never reads as waiting on a person, which is the signal's alone, and never as the brand.
- **Moving** (`move`, `move-ink`, `move-tint`): in-progress work, active goals and agent claims. It is the brand, by reference, so moving work and the brand are one hue.
- **Arrived green** (`arrive`, `arrive-ink`, `arrive-tint`): done tasks, achieved goals, active agents.
- **The five stations**: inbox is the sky (`station-inbox`), backlog a quiet grey (`station-backlog`), to do a rose or orchid (`station-todo`: Dusk's own dusk rose, oklch(71% 0.12 330)), set well apart from backlog in hue, chroma and lightness and from in progress in hue, in progress the brand (`station-progress`), and done green (`station-done`). Thin timetable lines use the darker cuts `line-inbox`, `line-backlog` and `line-todo`, which keep 3:1 against a card; backlog's line is dotted, since nothing is planned for it yet. A workspace's own stages take the colour of their kind: a "Review" stage inside In progress takes the brand. The contrast test holds every pair of inbox, to do, in progress and done at least 25 degrees of hue apart in every theme.

### Neutral

- **Ground** (`paper`): the page background (Dusk's bone in light, slate in dark), with a faint brand glow at the top left and a signal one at the top right (`glow-violet`, `glow-ember`, named for Ledger but set by each theme) for the glass to pick up.
- **Panel** (`panel`): cards, inputs, the sample timetable.
- **Fill** (`fill`): secondary buttons, segmented-control wells, hover fills, chart troughs, the future side of the now line, skeletons.
- **Raised** (`raised`): the selected segment of a segmented control; near white in light, a lifted grey of the ground in dark.
- **Hairline** (`rule`) and **Edge** (`edge`): dividers and card borders; `edge` outlines form controls.
- **Ink** (`ink`), **Ink 2** (`ink-2`), **Muted** (`muted`), **Faint** (`faint`): text from titles down to empty-state icons.
- **Dusk** (`dusk`, `dusk-2`, `dusk-ink`, `dusk-muted`): the marketing site's decisions band, and code blocks. The `dusk` utility is a sky that lightens from `dusk` at the top to `dusk-2` at the horizon, with no glow: on a dusk band the signal belongs to the boulder and the one call to action. In every theme `dusk` is dark and `dusk-ink` light, at 7:1 or more, because the QR code for two-factor (`components/account/qr-code.tsx`) draws `dusk` modules on `dusk-ink` and must stay dark on light to scan.

### Themes

A theme is a named set of every token above, with a light and a dark value for each, selected by `<html data-theme>`. Four ship, each adapted from a Hallmark theme to Phean's roles (only their colours: the type stays Phean's in every theme):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dusk` (default) | Dusk | bone oklch(95.5% 0.008 75) · slate oklch(17.5% 0.014 268) | periwinkle 274 | apricot 60 | misty blue 222 · dusk rose 330 · sage 160 |
| `ledger` | none (Phean's first, custom theme), value for value | lavender oklch(97.3% 0.008 300) · violet night oklch(16.5% 0.022 292) | violet 292 | ember 50 | sky 232 · orchid 322 · green 158 |

**How a theme reaches the page.** The `phean-theme` cookie is read on the server by `getTheme()` in `server/web.ts`, as the appearance is by `getAppearance()`, and the root layout renders it as `<html data-theme>` beside `data-appearance`, so the first paint is already in both. `themes.css` sets each theme's light tokens under `[data-theme="…"]` and its dark ones under `[data-appearance="dark"]`, or under System (or no choice) on a device that prefers dark. The rules match `[data-theme]` on any element too, so the picker's swatches (`ThemeSwatch`) show each theme's real colours in the appearance in force. `lib/themes.ts` holds what the client shares: the ids, their names and descriptions, what a swatch shows, and each theme's paper in sRGB for the `theme-color` meta. The picker (`ThemePicker`, in the account menu, the Account page's Appearance card, and the landing page's footer as a compact row) is a group of radios: each a swatch and a name (the swatch's `size`: `sm` in the footer, `md` in the menu, and `lg` on the Account page, where four cards in a row each show a small picture of the app in that theme: the bar with the ember and the selected tab, and a card with a line of text, the four stations and a brand button), the selected one ringed in the brand, marked with a check and `data-selected`, so the choice never rests on colour alone; arrow keys move between them and a choice applies at once, with the cookie and the metas, and every other picker on the page follows.

**Adding a theme.** Add its id to `THEMES` with a name and description in `THEME_META` (`lib/themes.ts`); give it a light and a dark palette in `THEME_TOKENS` (`lib/theme-tokens.ts`), every token, deriving dark by `references/dark-mode.md`'s recipe (ground at 12 to 18% L, elevation lighter, the hue never moving); run `pnpm themes`; add its paper to `THEME_PAPER` (the test prints the sRGB); then run `pnpm test`, which fails until the stylesheet matches and every text pair holds 4.5:1, every line and mark 3:1, red stays red, the signal warm, the stations apart and the QR code dark on light. `e2e/quality.spec.ts` scans the key pages in it in both appearances. Then add its row to the table above.

### Dark appearance

Every token is redefined for dark in every theme, which holds when `<html data-appearance>` is `dark`, or when it is `system` (or missing) and the device prefers dark. The attribute comes from the `phean-appearance` cookie, read by `getAppearance()` in the root layout, so the first paint is already in the chosen appearance; the picker sets the attribute and the cookie together, and the change is instant. In Dusk the ground drops to slate at 17.5%, panels to 21% and fills to 27.5%, on hue 268; text turns to a bone white; each `-ink` lifts to a light cut of its hue; each `-tint` becomes a deep wash of its hue around 28 to 30% lightness; and the brand lightens to a periwinkle that takes dark labels. Glass turns to smoked slate with a light rim. The `theme-color` and `color-scheme` metas follow the choice: the theme's paper in each scheme (Dusk `#f3efea` and `#0e1017`), and one of each, by media query, under System.

### Named Rules

**The Every Theme Rule.** A colour is a token with a light and a dark value in every theme, never a literal in a component, and never a theme's name in a component. Every text pair holds 4.5:1 and every line and control 3:1 in every theme and both appearances; `tests/themes.test.ts` computes them from the tokens, the axe scans in `e2e/quality.spec.ts` run the default theme in both appearances and every other theme on the key pages, and the pickers' tests in `e2e/appearance.spec.ts` and `e2e/themes.spec.ts` guard the switch.

**The Prominence Rule.** A view has one or two filled brand actions at most. The forward step repeated on every row of a list (Done on each task) is tinted, and the rest are secondary or ghost.

**The One Ember Rule.** The signal (ember, in any theme's hue) marks what needs a person: the boulder, the counts on the rail, what waits on someone's answer or approval, and one call to action on a dusk band. New work in the inbox is sky, not ember. Ember is never a panel fill and never body text.

**The Station Rule.** A station hue means that status everywhere: in the flow bar, the rail atop each board column, the cumulative flow and the timetable. Nothing else borrows those hues except the brand, which in-progress shares on purpose.

**The Red Line Rule.** Red is the now line and late work, plus urgent priority and destructive actions. If something is red, a person should look at it today.

**The One Meaning Rule.** A word means one thing on every screen. "Decision" is an agent's request for a person's call; a goal waiting on a person is an approval; the memory kind recorded as `decision` reads "Choice". Kinds are categories, not states, so memory kinds stay neutral (facts take the info tint), and the owner's role badge is info, never ember.

## Typography

**Display Font:** SF Pro on Apple devices (`-apple-system`), Inter Variable elsewhere
**Body Font:** the same
**Figures:** SF Pro Rounded (`ui-rounded`) on Apple devices, Inter Variable elsewhere
**Label/Mono Font:** ui-monospace (SF Mono on Apple devices) for tokens, digests and code

**Character:** Apple's own grotesque where the system has it, and Inter, its closest open cousin, where it does not, with optical sizing on so titles tighten and small text opens up. Only WebKit, which knows Apple's text styles, gets SF Pro: the switch sits behind `@supports (font: -apple-system-body)`, because other engines map `-apple-system` to their own UI face. Figures go rounded on Apple devices, like the numerals in Apple's own dashboards. All numerals are tabular, so columns of figures line up.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (Bricolage Grotesque, 700, `--tracking-hero`): the marketing site's headlines (see Marketing site). Every page title in the app (`h1`) also takes the display face, at its own size and `--tracking-title`, so the app and the site read as one.
- **Display panel** (700, 2.5rem, 1.08, -0.028em): the dusk panel's statement beside sign-in and sign-up, on large screens.
- **Headline** (700, 1.75rem, 2.15rem, -0.016em): the page title, one per screen. The auth, invitation, error and not-found pages use 1.375rem.
- **Title** (600, 1rem, 1.55rem): card and section titles.
- **Figure** (700, 1.375rem, rounded, tabular, -0.016em): stat values and due dates in lead columns, through the `figure` utility.
- **Body** (400, 0.875rem, 1.35rem): running text and table cells; descriptions stay under about 70ch.
- **Label** (600, 0.75rem, 1.1rem): buttons, field labels, badges, tab names. Sentence case, never uppercase.
- **Axis** (400, 0.6875rem, 1rem): chart axes, track notes and the Now chip.

### Named Rules

**The Three Weights Rule.** 400 for running text, 600 for labels and titles, 700 for page titles, figures and the display. Nothing heavier.

## Layout

Workspace screens sit in an 80rem container with 16px gutters on phones and 24px from the small breakpoint. The header is a 56px row (wordmark, switcher, account) with the tab rail below it, and it stays on top as the page scrolls. From the large breakpoint (1024px) the overview and goal pages use three columns: the main column spans two (questions and decisions, the line, next actions, the goal map) and a rail holds late work, agents and activity. Cards stack 24px apart and pad their content 20px from the edge; card headers are a title, an optional description and one action on the right. Page and card descriptions fold behind a small (?) beside the title (`InfoTip`): hovering or focusing it shows the text in a glass tooltip, clicking pins it open (the only way on a touchscreen), and Escape or a click elsewhere closes it; screen readers hear the text as the button's description, so headers take one line.

On phones both columns dissolve into a single stack ordered by what needs a person first: the questions card, the decision card, late work, the line, next actions, the goal map, agents, activity. Wide tables keep their first column pinned while the figures scroll beside it inside the card. Popovers and the workspace switcher open as a glass sheet across the top of the screen, over the header. The timetable puts each task's label above its track. Rails of chips (the tab rail, the board's lane picker, the layout switcher) are `chip-rail`s: they scroll sideways with no scrollbar, fade at whichever edge has more beyond it, and bring the selected chip to the middle (`useChipRail` in `components/ui/chip-rail.tsx`). Scrollbars take the quiet `faint` colour only where there is a mouse; touch screens keep their own overlay indicators.

## Elevation & Depth

Two layers, as in Apple's Liquid Glass: content and the functional layer above it. The content layer is flat, and its depth is tonal: lighter panels on the tinted ground, filled wells and hover fills, the shaded future past the now line. The functional layer is glass: translucent, blurred and saturated, with a hairline rim, so it reads as floating over the content rather than as another band of colour.

### Glass Vocabulary

- **Bar** (`glass-bar`: `glass` fill, `backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(1.6)`, an inset hairline at the bottom in `glass-rim`): the sticky header. The fill sits on a `::before` so the header is not a backdrop root and the switcher inside it can blur the page. Below it, a 1rem scroll edge fades the content (`glass-fade` to transparent) instead of a hard line.
- **Float** (`glass-float`: `glass-thick` fill, `blur(28px) saturate(1.6)`, an inset rim, an inset top sheen in `glass-sheen`, and the float shadow): the workspace switcher, popovers and the phone sheet. Thicker, because these hold text and forms.
- **Fallbacks**: without `backdrop-filter`, under `prefers-reduced-transparency`, or under `prefers-contrast: more`, glass becomes solid `panel`; under increased contrast the rim becomes `edge`, and the scroll edge is removed.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Float** (`box-shadow: 0 18px 40px -16px oklch(22% 0.06 292 / 0.3), 0 3px 10px -3px oklch(22% 0.06 292 / 0.14)`; dark `0 18px 40px -16px oklch(8% 0.02 292 / 0.6)`): glass menus and popovers, the only things that float, and the cumulative flow's tooltip.
- **Raised** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px oklch(22% 0.04 295 / 0.14), 0 1px 1px -0.5px oklch(22% 0.04 295 / 0.08)`; none in dark): the selected segment of a segmented control.
- **Lift** (`--shadow-lift`: a long soft drop under a hairline ring; dark a deeper drop and a faint light ring): the marketing site's product frames only, the pictures of the app that float over the page. Nothing in the app lifts.

### Named Rules

**The Functional Layer Rule.** Glass is for navigation and controls that float above the content: the header, the switcher, popovers and the phone sheet. Cards, lanes, tables and charts are never glass, and nothing that is content sits on glass.

**The Flat Content Rule.** Cards, task cards and tables never cast a shadow, and a card never sits inside another card. If something needs to stand out, give it a state tone, not elevation. The surface ladder is tonal: `paper` (the ground) → `panel` (cards, through `surface`, with its lit top edge in dark) → `raised` (the selected segment), with `fill` for wells and hovers inside a panel. A board card's hairline deepens toward `edge` on hover.

## Shapes

Controls are capsules: buttons, tabs, badges, count chips, segmented controls and the workspace switcher are fully rounded. Containers are rounded rectangles that grow with their size: 4px for chart bars, chips and the Now flag, 10px for inputs, task cards, menu rows and code blocks, 14px for cards, 16px for menus, popovers and sheets. Round shapes also belong to the line: the hollow dot where a track sets out, the green dot where it arrives, the stations on the goal map, and the boulder. Each board column is headed by a 4px capsule rail in its station colour, and its task cards stand on the ground beneath it rather than in a box; a dropped track ends in a short slash.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** capsules, 36px tall; small buttons are 32px with a hit area extended to 44px.
- **Pressed:** a capsule gives a little under the finger (scaled to 97% for 120ms, motion-safe only, never while disabled); menu rows and agents' long answers (`choice`) stay still.
- **Primary:** the brand with `on-accent` text, `accent-2` on hover. One or two per view (see The Prominence Rule).
- **Tinted:** a brand wash with brand text, for the forward step repeated on each row (Done).
- **Secondary:** a filled grey capsule, a shade deeper on hover.
- **Ghost:** text only, for the secondary actions in a row (Release, Drop, Open board, Mark answered), the account menu's trigger and its Sign out, and every manual entry point (Add manually, Add task, Add sub-goal).
- **Danger:** crimson text on a crimson wash, and it always asks for confirmation.
- **Menu rows:** a full-width ghost row inside a `⋯` menu (`Button` size `menu`), icon first, for the rare moves of a task (Release, Drop) or a request (Answered in the conversation). The `⋯` trigger is a ghost capsule that is always visible and is named "More actions for …".

**The Undo or Confirm Rule.** Frequent, reversible actions run at once and offer Undo: Drop, Done, Reopen, lane moves and answers say what they did in the status region ("Dropped “Read the thread”" · Undo), and Undo runs the reverse transition that already exists. Rare actions with consequences ask first: Revoke, Forget, Abandon, Remove, Mark answered, and Mark achieved while tasks are still open. If everything asked, people would learn to click through.
- **Signal:** ember with dark text, used at most once per dusk band.
- **Dusk:** quiet text controls on a dusk band, lifting to `dusk-2` on hover.
- **Focus:** a 2px `focus` outline (the brand) offset 2px; on a dusk band the ring turns ember.

**The Agents First Rule.** Agents fill the ledger, so adding a task or goal by hand is always a ghost button, never primary or secondary, and never a form laid open on the page. It sits in the page header or a card header and opens a panel. Connecting an agent is what a new workspace is asked to do first.

### Chips

- **Style:** badges are 20px capsules, a tint of their tone with the tone's ink. Every status, priority, role and memory kind renders through its `{ label, tone }` table in `src/lib/domain.ts`.
- **Counts:** the tab rail's counts for what needs a person (Overview, Decisions) are ember capsules with dark numerals; the Tasks tab's inbox count is a neutral capsule (`fill`, `ink-2`), so ember keeps its meaning.
- **Filters:** the memory kinds are capsules, brand-tinted when selected and filled grey otherwise, each with its count.
- **Lane counts:** a neutral capsule beside the lane's name; a folded lane reads "5 of 13".
- **Who acted:** an agent is a squared tile with a bot, a person a round disc, both neutral and ringed in a hairline so they hold their shape on any surface; a task's operator is meta text with a bot or person glyph, "Operator: Finance clerk", never a violet badge, since violet is a state. A handover under way is a badge with a handshake, in `DELEGATION_META`'s tones: handing off is sky (new to whoever picks it up), picking up is the violet of work in motion, returned is neutral; never ember, which stays for what waits on a person's answer. The handoff note sits under the title on the task's page as a quiet figure, with its age. A board card being handed off takes a dashed `line-inbox` edge, so work in transit reads by its outline as well as its badge (late work keeps its late edge). The overview's Handoffs card, in the right column after Late, lists handovers with the viewer's first, marked "For you" in words, each with from and to, its age, the note's first lines, and a tinted Take it.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner Style:** 14px.
- **Background:** panel, through the `surface` utility (globals.css), which in dark adds a hairline of light along the top edge (`glass-sheen`, inset) so the step from the ground to the panel reads without a heavier border. The question and decision cards are ember and the late card crimson: a softened border (the tone at 45% and 30%), and a header that is a quiet wash of the tone's tint over the panel (55% and 60%), lit along its top edge by a 2px inset line in the tone itself, never a full band of colour (Dusk's signals are never a large fill). A tinted header's description takes the tone's ink rather than grey; the wash sits between the tint and the panel, so that ink holds its contrast in every theme. Card titles take `--tracking-title`.
- **Shadow Strategy:** none (see Elevation & Depth); the dark top edge is an inset line, not a lift.
- **Border:** one hairline.
- **Internal Padding:** 20px; the header is ruled off from the body.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** panel, a 1px edge border, 10px corners, 36px tall, full width of their wrapper (size the wrapper, not the control).
- **Focus:** the border turns to the brand and a soft 3px brand halo grows around it, as on Apple's text fields.
- **Error / Disabled:** errors show inline under the form in late ink; disabled controls go filled with muted text.

### Empty states

A quiet centred block: an icon in a 40px `fill` disc in `muted`, a semibold title, and one muted line saying what will fill the space and how. Two empty states are moments rather than gaps, and carry a small spot illustration instead of the icon (`EmptyState` with `art`, decorative, 7rem wide): **All clear** on Decisions when nothing waits on the person (`spot-boulder`), and **No agents yet** on the Agents page (`spot-signal`, an agent sending its first signal). Never on a working surface, a card in a list, or more than one per view.

### Segmented controls

A grey capsule well with capsule segments; the selected one is raised (near white with the raised shadow in light, a lifted grey in dark). Used for the timeline window (14, 30 or 90 days), the MCP client tabs in Connect an agent, the task or goal choice in manual entry, and the Light, Dark and System choice of the appearance picker, whose segments carry a sun, a moon and a display.

### Navigation

The glass header holds the boulder wordmark, a `/`, the workspace switcher (a capsule that opens a glass menu), the search box (from 768px; phones use the Search tab), and the account menu: a ghost capsule with the person's initial on a brand-tinted disc and their name (the initial alone on phones), which opens a glass popover with their name and email, the theme picker, the appearance picker, and Sign out. Below them, the tab rail (Overview, Decisions, Tasks, Goals, Timeline, Memory, Documents, Search, Agents, Members, from `WORKSPACE_PAGES` in `lib/workspace-pages.ts`) is a row of capsules: muted until hovered, which fills them grey; the active tab is a violet-tinted capsule with semibold violet text. Inline text links underline on hover and thicken the underline while pressed (the `link` utility).

### Menus, popovers and sheets

Glass floats with a 16px corner that grow in from their anchor (220ms, from 97% scale and 4px up). A panel takes focus when it opens (its first field, or itself), opens upward when there is no room below its trigger, and gives focus back to the trigger when Escape closes it; an open trigger keeps its fill. A form panel has a small heading ("Edit task") and a ghost Cancel beside its primary button, closes itself on success and reports in the status region. On phones panels become a sheet at the bottom of the screen, 12px from each edge, so what they act on stays in view above them, and scroll inside themselves when taller than the screen; the answer to an agent's question opens inline under the question instead.

### Status region

One polite `role="status"` region per workspace, a glass float at the bottom centre of the screen. Actions say what they did there, naming the object, with Undo when the reverse exists; a notice stays about six seconds, longer while it is hovered or holds focus, and Undo is reachable with Tab. The background refresh speaks there too, only when something new waits on the person, and at most once a minute.

### Tasks layouts

The Tasks page draws the same tasks four ways, chosen in a segmented control of links with icons (Board, Table, Calendar, Map), each layout its own URL (`?view=`), with the Goal and Status filters (`ToolbarSelect` capsules, through the shared `QueryFilter`) beside it and carried from layout to layout.
- **Table**: a card holding a full-width table, the first column pinned on phones (`PINNED_CELL`). Headings are links that sort (`aria-sort` and a small arrow on the leading one); empty cells read "None" or "No one" in muted text; the due date reads as everywhere else (`InlineDue`, crimson when late).
- **Calendar**: a month as a ruled table, Monday first, days outside the month on a `fill` wash, today ringed in late crimson (the Red Line Rule's now), each task a station-coloured dot and its title, late ones in late ink, three a day before "N more". Phones get a list of the days with tasks instead of the grid.
- **Map**: a left-to-right tree on a card under the station key, goals as panel boxes with the goal map's status marks (and their status in words unless active), tasks as panel boxes with their station dot, titles on up to two lines, "Decision for you" in wait ink and "Late" in late ink, joined by `edge` curves; it scrolls inside its card, a screen tall at most. The boxes are a nested list, so the hierarchy is read aloud, and the curves are decorative; below 640px the same list is an indented outline.

Across the table, calendar and map, a task's station dot names its status for screen readers (`StationDot`, with an `edge` ring so the pale stations hold 3:1), what waits on a person keeps its ember mark, and the task open in the side peek is marked selected (`TaskPeekLink`: `aria-current`, an accent outline or bar).

A status bar's legend (`FlowLegend` with `hrefFor`) turns each status into a link to its tasks as a table, and its segments into the same links for the pointer; the bar then hides itself from assistive tech, since the legend says the same.

### Search suggestions

The header's search and the Search page's box are one combobox (`SearchForm` with `suggestFor`): a pill input, and under it a `glass-float` list grouped under small muted headings (Go to, Show tasks, Goals, Tasks, Documents, Memory), each option a label with muted detail at its right (a page found by another word names the word), the highlighted one on `accent-tint` with an accent bar at its left edge, and a last row, ruled off, that searches for the words. Arrow keys move, Enter opens, Escape closes then clears; the count is announced politely.

### Board stages and Settings

The board's lanes are the workspace's stages, edited on the Settings page (from the workspace menu, never the tab rail, so configuration stays out of the daily view). Each stage takes its kind's station colour on its rail; a hidden lane that still holds tasks says "Hidden". More than four lanes scroll sideways on a wide screen rather than squeeze. Each lane splits into two rows, People above Agents, told apart by structure rather than colour: a caption per row (a person or bot icon, the row's name in sentence case, its count in muted figures), a hairline rule above Agents, and on a wide screen a subgrid that lines both rows up across the lanes so the captions and the rule read as one horizontal band. Rows are never tinted; ember stays for the cards that wait on a person. An empty row is a quiet dashed outline, and a segmented control in the header (People and agents, One row) turns the split off. A narrow card with Resume shows Edit as its pencil alone, so its actions stay on one line. A card's ⋯ menu lists every visible stage under "Move to", so a phone or a narrow lane can still go from Inbox straight to Done. Each lane's header (its rail, name and count) stays pinned as the lane scrolls, so a long People row never leaves the Agents row without its lane's name: under the glass bar on the page, or at the top of the board itself where more than four lanes make the board its own scroller, a screen tall, on wide screens.

### Documents

A document reads like a page of the ledger: its title as the page's headline (Markdown headings sit a level below it), Markdown set in the body face with token colours, tables ruled in hairlines, code on `fill`, and Mermaid diagrams drawn on a panel in the appearance's own theme, with their source one click away. On a task, each document is a quiet `fill` chip with a document icon and no arrow, since it stays inside Phean. The editor is a monospace field with a live preview beside it on wide screens and a Write and Preview switch on phones.

### Peeks

A task or a document opens in a peek over the page it was opened from, never in place of it. A task opens as a **side peek** at the right edge, so the board or list stays in view beside it (the way Notion's side peek opens database items, and because Jira's users pushed back when an overlay hid the list they were triaging). A document opens as a **centre peek**, wide enough to read, with **Full screen** to fill the window for long reading or editing. Every peek keeps the item's own URL: Back closes it, a refresh or a shared link opens the item's full page, and **Open as page** goes there on purpose. A peek is a native modal dialog: the page behind is inert under a light ink scrim, focus moves into the peek and returns to what opened it, and Escape closes it. Its toolbar is a glass bar; its content sits on the ground, never on glass. On phones a peek is the whole screen.

### Selection

What is selected must never be a guess. The selected request on Decisions and the overview (the one holding focus, or the one J and K moved to) carries an accent bar at its left edge, an accent tint, and `aria-current`; a board card whose peek is open takes an accent outline and tint. Colour is never the only sign, and the selected item is scrolled into view as the selection moves.

Sources for these choices: Linear's [peek](https://linear.app/docs/peek.md); Notion's [open pages as side peek, centre peek or full page](https://www.notion.com/releases/2022-07-20); feedback on Jira's [side panel](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-Service-Management/Jira-Cloud-filter-results-now-open-issues-in-a-side-panel-can/qaa-p/3241087); the W3C [dialog (modal) pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialogmodal/).

### Keyboard shortcuts

`?` opens a legend of every shortcut as a glass sheet (it is also in the account menu). Keys are shown as small `kbd` capsules in a hairline outline. Shortcuts never fire while focus is in a field.

### Toolbar filters and search

A toolbar filter is a capsule at the height of the segmented control beside it, "Goal: All goals ▾", with a label for screen readers (`ToolbarSelect`); fields inside forms stay rectangular. A search box above a list is a pill with a clear button, and `/` focuses it from anywhere on the page (or opens Search). Matched words in results are marked with a violet wash (`accent-tint`), never the browser's yellow.

A popover's trigger is a small button, except where the panel leads elsewhere rather than acts: **Resume** uses the link trigger (`Popover look="link"`), brand semibold text with its icon, underlined on hover, with the same 44px hit area as a small button. It sits at the end of a task card's action row, after the state actions, and never wraps onto a line of its own in a board lane. Its panel lists each agent's session: the agent, its app and when it linked, the brief, a tinted **Resume in …** link, and the ids and command as monospace values, cut to one line, each with a ghost copy button named for what it copies ("Copy session id").

### Outside links

Everything that leaves Phean goes through `OutsideLink` and ends with an up-right arrow icon, the one arrow a control may carry. Web links open in a new tab, with `rel="noopener noreferrer"` and "(opens in a new tab)" for screen readers; app links (`cursor://`, `slack://`) open their app in place. On the overview, the asking agent's **Resume in Claude** is the entry's primary button, because answering in the agent's own conversation is the quickest way through; a task's source label ("From email") becomes a quiet outside link when the agent recorded a web link to the message.

### Logo

The mark is drawn in `src/components/logo.tsx` from one `MARK` geometry on a 32-unit square: a concave hill rising from the bottom left to the top right edge, and a boulder (a circle of radius 6.4 at 12.65, 19.17) resting on the slope partway up. The boulder is ember, lit from the upper left (`signal`) with a crescent of shade on its downhill side (`signal-shade`). The wordmark sets "Phean" beside it in 700 at title tracking. The favicon (`src/app/icon.svg`) and the Apple touch icon (`src/app/apple-icon.tsx`) put the same mark on a dusk tile, a gradient from `icon-tile-top` (`#3b296b`) to `icon-tile-bottom` (`#211541`), with Ledger's dark hill and boulder colours and the slope carried past the tile's edges. The icons, the favicon badge and the digest email are the brand mark, fixed in Ledger's colours whatever theme a person picks, since a browser tab, a home screen and a mail client never see the theme. The favicon adds a faint white rim (30% opacity, 1.2 units) so the tile keeps its square on dark tab strips, where the dusk ground would otherwise melt into the chrome. Inside a workspace, `components/favicon-badge.tsx` redraws the favicon so the boulder carries a count, the way mail apps show unread mail: a larger boulder in the icon's own ember, lit from the upper left, drawn where the original rests and covering it, so there is one boulder, with the rim drawn again over it, with dark figures counting decisions, approvals and inbox tasks (the tab rail's Decisions and Board counts). Past 99 it stretches into a pebble reading "99+", and at zero the plain mark returns. It is drawn into the page's own icon links, because browsers pick icons by size and type, so an added PNG would lose to the SVG.

### Marketing site (signature)

The landing (`app/page.tsx`, `components/marketing/`) is the one Persuade surface, so it is louder than the app while speaking its language. The message is the product's: **task management, flipped**. Agents capture, track and finish the work, people make the calls, and a fleet of agents needs one place to ask. The name is explained once, in its own section: Phean is short for *Sisyphean*, and the boulder that rolls back every morning is now the agents' to push. Personal assistants are named as the agents Phean helps most.

- **World:** light first, at the calm end of the day (Hallmark's Dusk). The hero is an *hour*: the commissioned illustration of three agents rolling the ember boulder up a violet hill (`public/marketing`, `hero-light` and `hero-dark`, both rendered and one shown by `art-light`/`art-dark` on `data-appearance`, so a change of appearance swaps it in the same frame and the hidden one, lazy, is never fetched), full-bleed behind the head from 1024px with a paper fog behind the words (`hero-fog`) and its foot fading to the ground; on a phone it sits under the words. The animated Decisions screen rises out of that foot. Two dusk bands carry the name and the decisions, and the close is a third, its hills (`cta-band`) under a scrim of `dusk` (`cta-scrim`) so every word holds 4.5:1. A turning conic glow (`data-glow`, the brand hues blurred, fainter in dark) lights the hero's picture of the product from behind. The `brand-mesh` remains in the stylesheet but the site no longer uses it.
- **Illustrations:** one series, violet and ember, served by `Art` (`components/marketing/art.tsx`) as `<picture>` with the pre-encoded AVIF and WebP widths, the largest file's width and height (no layout shift), lazy below the fold, and the alt text from `manifest.json` (empty for `cta-band`, and for the auth panel, where the headline says it). Where they go: capture beside "Task management, flipped", assistants behind the phone in the assistants section, decisions beside the decisions band's intro, memory and hearback across the top of two feature tiles. The `art` class tunes them per theme through `--art-tone` (none in Dusk and Ledger, whose hues they are; most of the colour taken out in Ink and Console, the ember still warm) and dims the light-ground ones in dark (`--art-dim`). The OG and Twitter card is `og-card-1200x630.jpg`.
- **Agent marks:** `components/marketing/agent-logo.tsx` holds one table of the agents the site names (name, docs page, group, mark) and draws each mark as one `currentColor` path, decorative beside the visible name: Claude, Claude Code and Cursor from Simple Icons (CC0), VS Code from Microsoft's Codicons (CC BY 4.0, credited under the strip), ChatGPT as the OpenAI mark OpenAI publishes in its own repository, Grok and Muse from Lobe Icons (MIT). Instinct publishes no usable mark, so it shows a monogram chip (the initial in a hairline ring, drawn by CSS so it is not text). Never redraw, recolour beyond `currentColor`, or invent a mark. The docs' Agents table shows the same marks (`MarkdownView` with `agentMarks`).
- **Type:** the display face, Bricolage Grotesque (`--font-display`, loaded by `next/font/local` with a metric-matched fallback; the `display` utility: 700, `--tracking-hero` -0.035em, 1.02 leading, balanced), sets headlines at clamp(2.75rem, 5vw, 4.5rem) in the hero and clamp(2.125rem, 4.2vw, 3.5rem) for sections: heads stay modest, as in Dusk, and the hour and the product are the loud parts. The hero's second sentence is solid `accent-ink`; there is no gradient text, no eyebrow or kicker above a heading, and no arrow glued to a button label. Ledes are `text-lg` in `ink-2`. Calls to action use `Button` size `lg`.
- **Product frames:** pictures of the app, never screenshots. `ProductFrame` wraps the app's real components (badges, avatars, station dots, the timetable) in a miniature glass header and tab rail, rounded `xl` on the `--shadow-lift`. On the site only, product frames float and overlap, since they are pictures held up to the page; in the app, cards stay flat.
- **The name, in clay:** the `#name` band's scene is three.js (`components/marketing/hill-scene.tsx`, `hill-scene-three.ts` and the modules in `components/marketing/hill/`): the hero illustration come to life. The framing is the hero's: one long violet slope rising from the lower left to a crest on the right, the worn path winding up its face (the crew walks it), ridges of far hills paling into the haze at the lower left, and the last warm light along the horizon under a clear dusk sky with a few stars. The boulder is the lamp: a big dented stone, wider than an agent is tall, whose plates glow deep ember and whose cracks run hotter, with a warm point light at its heart, a halo, a pool of light on the ground and a soft contact shadow. The world is painted (textured, softly lit); **the characters are cel-shaded**, as anime sets figures on a painted background: flat steps of light from one ramp (`toonRamp`) and a dark ink line round every shape (each mesh drawn again, swollen and inside out). **The agents are robots** of the illustrations' family: pale lavender shells with pressed panel seams and screws, a dark visor with two LED eyes that blink, an antenna with a lit tip that sways, thin struts for upper arms and thighs, chunky forearms ending in mittens, boots, a pack, and trim in a station hue each (`station-todo`, `station-done`, `station-inbox`); they differ in size and antenna so they read as a fleet. They push the boulder in from below the frame's lower left, nose to tail as the hero draws them, and they touch without overlapping: the lead stands square to the stone, mittens on its own dented surface, and each one behind stands straight behind the pack ahead with its mittens on it. Where each stands is worked out afresh every frame from the loop's progress alone (`hill/crew.ts`: no frame remembers the one before, so the hundredth loop is the first, and `tests/hill.test.ts` holds it to that and to nobody being inside the boulder or each other). They lean in, step with alternating feet as far as the stone has rolled, glance round at us, stand back at the top, throw their arms up when the call is made, then turn round by our side and walk back down the path, arms swinging, and out of the frame. **The person is an anime character, a model not our own**: VRoid's AvatarSample_C (pixiv's VRoid Project; used under VRoid's conditions of use, not CC0: see `public/marketing/models/README.md`), a figure with dark hair in a dark jacket with a grey front, dark trousers and boots. It ships as `public/marketing/models/avatar-sample-c.glb`, cut down for this size (512px colour maps, three faces, meshopt-packed, about 580 KB) and fetched only by the scene, after the scene's own chunk. Its skin, clothes and hair take the scene's cel shading and ink line, as the bots do; the features of the face stay unlit, as on a cel; its colours are its textures' own, the one thing in the scene the theme does not tint (the theme's lights still fall on it). It is posed on its own skeleton from rest every frame (`hill/avatar.ts`): it stands easy, feet hip-width apart with the toes a little out, the weight on one leg and the other knee soft (the legs bend to feet that stay planted while the hips shift over them); it breathes, blinks and turns its head to watch the crew climb. The phone lies in its right palm, the fingers across the back and round the far edge, the thumb on the glass: low at its side, then before its eyes to read, the screen square to them a forearm away, the elbow in; it taps with the tip of the left index finger on the glass, smiles, shuts its eyes in the smile as the boulder goes, and holds the lit phone up for the crew, then lowers it and watches them leave. Hair and clothes do not swing. **The loop plays only while it is watched.** Its clock (and the card's animations with it) waits at its first moment until at least 40% of the figure is in view, so the visitor sees the first loop from its first frame, the empty hill; it stops whenever the figure is off screen or the tab is hidden, and goes on from there when it is seen again (`data-playing` on the figure, which the CSS needs before anything runs; with no script at all the card simply loops). Until then the scene shows its first frame, still. **From the third loop on, every call is the visitor's**: the scene plays through twice by itself, to show how it goes, and the third time the crew reaches the top, and every time after, the clock stops at the question (`hill/timeline.ts`, counted on the loop's own clock, so nothing unseen skips a wait or counts a loop). The scene idles there, the decision card stops being decoration and becomes theirs (`HillCard`: real buttons, "Ship it" ringed in `signal-ink` with a ring that breathes, and a line that says "Your call: ship it, or hold a week?"), and when they answer, by click or key, the tap happens and the loop goes on from that moment. **While it waits, the crew looks where the visitor points**: each bot's head turns to the pointer over the figure (the last touch, on a touch screen), or to the button that has the keyboard's focus, within what a neck allows, and back to the person when the pointer leaves. The pointer is an input like the clock: `hill-scene.tsx` eases where and how much they attend (`approach`, a tested step), and the head's turn is a pure matter of the pose and the point (`gaze` in `hill/bot.ts`). Either answer is the call made, the card shows which, and each ends the loop its own way (`branchAt` in `hill/timeline.ts`, `scoreAt`'s branch in `hill/crew.ts`). **Ship it**, and every loop the visitor does not answer, is the boulder leaving in light. **Hold a week** is Sisyphus's own ending: the person shrugs and lowers the phone, the crew steps off the path, and the boulder rolls back down it, slowly and then fast, turning as far as it travels with its light and its shadow going with it, out of the frame the way it came in; the crew turns and follows it down. Both end on the same empty hill. Under 768px, where the card has no room in the picture, it shows only for that turn. With reduced motion, and where the drawing stands in for the scene, nothing waits and nothing follows the pointer. **No two boulders are quite alike**: each loop's is one of three stones (their plates and cracks cut from different rock, baked when the scene loads), up to a tenth larger or smaller, and a little deeper or paler within the ember's colours, all decided by the loop's number alone (`boulderOf` in `hill/cast.ts`); the crew's hands, their clearance and both endings are worked out for its size. **The boulder leaves when the call is made**: it gathers light and lifts, a column of light strikes up from it with a flash, it stretches up the column and is gone, a ripple runs out over the ground and embers drift up and die, all in `signal` warmed towards `dusk-ink`. Its light comes up at the start while it is still out of the frame, so the loop ends and begins on the same empty hill and cycles without a seam. Surfaces are textured, never flat: every map is painted in the browser when the scene loads (noise for the stone's plates, cracks and grain; the ground's chalk and its path; the shells' panels), grey so the theme tints it; the model is the only thing fetched. The canvas is transparent over the band's own gradient and fades into it at its edges (`hill-canvas`). **While it loads, a still drawing of its first frame stands in for it**, and nothing moves: the SVG in `boulder-hill.tsx` is the empty hill and the person at the crest (traced from the scene's own first frame, pose and all, in tones of the band's tokens), drawn on the server through the scene's own camera (`hill/view.ts`) from the scene's own hill (`hill/terrain.ts`) in the same mixes of the same tokens, so it lines up with the canvas that cross-fades over it, follows every theme, and costs no image and no script. three.js and the model load only when the figure comes within 400px of the screen, never in the first load, and the scene paints its textures a step at a time so loading never holds a frame for long. Wherever there is no WebGL or only WebGL drawn in software (SwiftShader, llvmpipe: it would hold the page for seconds to start and for every frame after), three.js is never fetched and the drawing stays, showing the call instead of the empty hill (`data-call`: the boulder at the top, the crew stood back, the answer held up), as it does before the scene arrives when motion is off; `?scene=3d` draws the scene there all the same, for looking at it in a headless browser. ## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** use the token names (`bg-panel`, `text-ink-2`, `border-rule`, `bg-accent`, `text-on-accent`, `bg-station-progress`) and nothing from Tailwind's default palette.
- **Do** check a new colour pair in both appearances: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for lines and controls.
- **Do** use `glass-bar` or `glass-float` for anything in the functional layer, so the fallbacks for reduced transparency and increased contrast come with it.
- **Do** give every chart a text equivalent: a `role="img"` name built with `describeCounts`, track notes in words, or a "Show the numbers" table.
- **Do** use the `link` utility for inline text links so they have hover and pressed states, and the `figure` utility for stat values.
- **Do** pin the first column of a wide table with `PINNED_CELL` from `src/components/ui/table.ts`, and make its scroller `relative`.
- **Do** put motion behind `motion-safe:` and keep it brisk: 120ms for colour changes, 220ms for menus and the timetable's dimming.
- **Do** route manual task and goal entry through `ManualEntry` (page headers) or a ghost `Popover` (card headers).
- **Do** add `data-*` hooks (`data-task`, `data-goal`, `data-segment`, `data-end`, `data-now`, `data-tick`) for UI tests instead of selecting by class.

### Don't:

- **Don't** set small labels in uppercase or letter-spaced; use sentence case at the label size.
- **Don't** put more than two filled brand buttons in a view, or ember anywhere but the boulder, the counts, the inbox and the dusk band's call to action.
- **Don't** make a card, board column, table or chart out of glass, or put glass inside a card.
- **Don't** use red for anything that is not late, now, urgent or destructive.
- **Don't** add shadows to cards, or gradients beyond the dusk band's sky and the ground's two faint top glows.
- **Don't** use a weight above 700 or a font other than the system face and Inter.
- **Don't** glue arrows to button text or put an eyebrow line above a heading. The up-right icon on an outside link is not decoration; it says the link leaves Phean.
- **Don't** clamp text over another element; shorten it (task cards cut descriptions at 80 characters and agent questions at 90, with a three-line clamp only as a guard) or move it.
