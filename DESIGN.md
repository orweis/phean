# Design System: Phean

The tokens above mirror the `@theme` block in `src/app/globals.css`, which is the source of truth. That block resets Tailwind's default palette (`--color-*: initial`), so a class like `text-zinc-500` does not exist here: every colour is one of these names. The `dark-` entries are the same tokens in the dark appearance, set once under the file's custom `dark` variant (`@variant dark` on `:root`), so components never name a dark colour themselves.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Decision Center"**

Agents do the tasks; people make the calls. The app is where those calls are made, so it stays quiet and exact, and the site is where the idea is sold, so it is bright and moving. The logo keeps the boulder on its violet slope. The marketing site explains the name once, in a drawing of agents rolling the boulder up the hill. The ledger itself is quiet. A lavender-white ground holds white cards ruled in hairlines, and the only material that floats is a Liquid Glass bar on top, in Apple's manner: the functional layer of navigation and menus sits on glass above the content and lets the content show through, blurred, as it scrolls beneath.

The density is that of an operations screen, not a marketing page. People check in a few times a day to see what agents did, what is late, and what awaits their decision or answer, so the overview leads with the line, the agents' questions and the decisions, and every figure is a tabular numeral with its label. Colour carries meaning only: violet is the brand and work in motion, ember is what is new and what needs a person, and each state has one hue on every screen.

Each person picks Light, Dark, or System (the default, which follows the device and changes with it) in the account menu, or in the landing page's footer before signing in. In dark the ground turns to a deep violet night and the glass to smoked violet; the hues lift to keep their contrast.

**Key Characteristics:**

- A sticky Liquid Glass header with the boulder wordmark, a workspace switcher and a capsule tab rail; content scrolls under it and fades at its edge.
- One brand hue, violet, for primary actions, the selected tab, focus and work in progress; one ember for the boulder, the counts on the rail and the call to action on a dusk band.
- Capsule controls throughout (buttons, tabs, badges, segmented controls); cards keep a 14px continuous-looking corner.
- SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter Variable with optical sizing everywhere else; SF Pro Rounded for figures where it exists.
- State is carried by line form as well as hue: dashed while waiting, solid while moving, a dot on arrival, a slash when dropped.
- Light, Dark, and System appearances, every text pair at 4.5:1 or better and every line and control at 3:1 or better in both palettes.

## Colors

A cool lavender ground under a violet brand, with one ember signal and four state hues that mean the same thing on every screen.

### Primary

- **Slope violet** (`accent`, oklch(50% 0.2 292) · dark oklch(55% 0.19 292)): the primary button, the active tab's capsule (as `accent-tint` with `accent-ink`), the selected workspace's check, the hero's gradient headline, focus rings (`focus`), and the in-progress station. `accent-2` is its hover. Text on it is `on-accent` (6.5:1 in light, 5.2:1 in dark); violet text on the ground is `accent-ink`.
- **The hill** (`hill`): the slope in the logo. It is the accent in light and a lighter cut, oklch(63% 0.17 292), in dark and on any dusk band (`dusk-hill`), so the mark holds on a dark ground.

### Secondary

- **Boulder ember** (`signal`, oklch(73% 0.165 50) · dark oklch(76% 0.15 52)): the boulder in the logo, the tab rail's counts of what needs a person, everything waiting on a person (as `wait`), the travelling requests in the marketing site's decisions band, and the one call to action on a dusk band. Labels on it are `on-signal`. `signal-shade` is the boulder's shaded side; `signal-ink` is ember text, used only in the accent badge on `signal-tint`.

### Tertiary

- **Late crimson** (`late`, `late-ink`, `late-tint`): the now line, late tracks and cards, urgent priority, and destructive buttons.
- **Waiting ember** (`wait`, `wait-ink`, `wait-tint`): what waits on a person, in the theme's own ember (goals awaiting approval, and agent questions: the Waiting on you badge, the question under it on a card, the rule beside it on the overview), high priority, read-only access. `wait` and `wait-tint` are the ember itself; `wait-ink` is a deeper cut for text, at 5.9:1 on its tint.
- **Inbox sky** (`inbox`, `inbox-ink`, `inbox-tint`): what just arrived and is still to triage: the inbox station, its line and its badge. A cool sky blue, so it never reads as waiting on a person, which is ember's alone.
- **Moving** (`move`, `move-ink`, `move-tint`): in-progress work, active goals and agent claims. It is the violet, by reference, so moving work and the brand are one hue.
- **Arrived green** (`arrive`, `arrive-ink`, `arrive-tint`): done tasks, achieved goals, active agents.
- **The five stations**: inbox is sky (`station-inbox`), backlog is a quiet grey (`station-backlog`), to do is lavender (`station-todo`), in progress is violet (`station-progress`), and done is green (`station-done`). Thin timetable lines use the darker cuts `line-inbox`, `line-backlog` and `line-todo`, which keep 3:1 against a card; backlog's line is dotted, since nothing is planned for it yet. A workspace's own stages take the colour of their kind: a "Review" stage inside In progress is violet.

### Neutral

- **Lavender ground** (`paper`): the page background, with a faint violet glow at the top left and an ember one at the top right (`glow-violet`, `glow-ember`) for the glass to pick up.
- **Panel** (`panel`): cards, inputs, the sample timetable.
- **Fill** (`fill`): secondary buttons, segmented-control wells, hover fills, chart troughs, the future side of the now line, skeletons.
- **Raised** (`raised`): the selected segment of a segmented control; white in light, a lifted violet-grey in dark.
- **Hairline** (`rule`) and **Edge** (`edge`): dividers and card borders; `edge` outlines form controls.
- **Ink** (`ink`), **Ink 2** (`ink-2`), **Muted** (`muted`), **Faint** (`faint`): text from titles down to empty-state icons.
- **Dusk** (`dusk`, `dusk-2`, `dusk-ink`, `dusk-muted`): the marketing site's decisions band, and code blocks. The `dusk` utility is a sky that lightens from `dusk` at the top to `dusk-2` at the horizon, with no glow: on a dusk band the ember belongs to the boulder and the one call to action.

### Dark appearance

Every token above is redefined under the custom `dark` variant, which holds when `<html data-appearance>` is `dark`, or when it is `system` (or missing) and the device prefers dark. The attribute comes from the `phean-appearance` cookie, read by `getAppearance()` in the root layout, so the first paint is already in the chosen appearance; the picker sets the attribute and the cookie together, and the change is instant. The ground drops to oklch(16.5% 0.022 292), panels to 21% and fills to 26.5%, all on the violet hue; text inverts to a lavender white; each `-ink` lifts to a light cut of its hue (violet ink to 77%, crimson to 77%, ember to 80%, sky to 82%, green to 79%); and each `-tint` becomes a deep wash of its hue around 28% lightness. Glass turns to smoked violet with a light rim. The `theme-color` and `color-scheme` metas follow the choice: `#f7f5fb` in light, `#0e0d17` in dark, and one of each, by media query, under System.

### Named Rules

**The Two Appearances Rule.** A colour is a token with a light and a dark value, never a literal in a component. Every text pair holds 4.5:1 and every line and control 3:1 in both appearances; the dark-scheme axe scans in `e2e/quality.spec.ts` and the picker's tests in `e2e/appearance.spec.ts` guard it.

**The Prominence Rule.** A view has one or two filled violet actions at most. The forward step repeated on every row of a list (Done on each task) is tinted, and the rest are secondary or ghost.

**The One Ember Rule.** Ember marks what needs a person: the boulder, the counts on the rail, what waits on someone's answer or approval, and one call to action on a dusk band. New work in the inbox is sky, not ember. Ember is never a panel fill and never body text.

**The Station Rule.** A station hue means that status everywhere: in the flow bar, the rail atop each board column, the cumulative flow and the timetable. Nothing else borrows those hues except the violet, which the brand and in-progress share on purpose.

**The Red Line Rule.** Red is the now line and late work, plus urgent priority and destructive actions. If something is red, a person should look at it today.

**The One Meaning Rule.** A word means one thing on every screen. "Decision" is an agent's request for a person's call; a goal waiting on a person is an approval; the memory kind recorded as `decision` reads "Choice". Kinds are categories, not states, so memory kinds stay neutral (facts take the info tint), and the owner's role badge is info, never ember.

## Typography

**Display Font:** SF Pro on Apple devices (`-apple-system`), Inter Variable elsewhere
**Body Font:** the same
**Figures:** SF Pro Rounded (`ui-rounded`) on Apple devices, Inter Variable elsewhere
**Label/Mono Font:** ui-monospace (SF Mono on Apple devices) for tokens, digests and code

**Character:** Apple's own grotesque where the system has it, and Inter, its closest open cousin, where it does not, with optical sizing on so titles tighten and small text opens up. Only WebKit, which knows Apple's text styles, gets SF Pro: the switch sits behind `@supports (font: -apple-system-body)`, because other engines map `-apple-system` to their own UI face. Figures go rounded on Apple devices, like the numerals in Apple's own dashboards. All numerals are tabular, so columns of figures line up.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (Bricolage Grotesque, 700, `--tracking-hero`): the marketing site's headlines (see Marketing site). Every page title in the app (`h1`) also takes the display face, at its own size and `--tracking-title`, so the app and the site read as one.
- **Display panel** (700, 2.5rem, 1.08, -0.028em): the dusk panel's statement beside sign-in and sign-up, on large screens.
- **Headline** (700, 1.75rem, 2.15rem, -0.016em): the page title, one per screen. The auth, invitation, error and not-found pages use 1.375rem.
- **Title** (600, 1rem, 1.55rem): card and section titles.
- **Figure** (700, 1.375rem, rounded, tabular, -0.016em): stat values and due dates in lead columns, through the `figure` utility.
- **Body** (400, 0.875rem, 1.35rem): running text and table cells; descriptions stay under about 70ch.
- **Label** (600, 0.75rem, 1.1rem): buttons, field labels, badges, tab names. Sentence case, never uppercase.
- **Axis** (400, 0.6875rem, 1rem): chart axes, track notes and the Now chip.

### Named Rules

**The Three Weights Rule.** 400 for running text, 600 for labels and titles, 700 for page titles, figures and the display. Nothing heavier.

## Layout

Workspace screens sit in an 80rem container with 16px gutters on phones and 24px from the small breakpoint. The header is a 56px row (wordmark, switcher, account) with the tab rail below it, and it stays on top as the page scrolls. From the large breakpoint (1024px) the overview and goal pages use three columns: the main column spans two (questions and decisions, the line, next actions, the goal map) and a rail holds late work, agents and activity. Cards stack 24px apart and pad their content 20px from the edge; card headers are a title, an optional description and one action on the right. Page and card descriptions fold behind a small (?) beside the title (`InfoTip`): hovering or focusing it shows the text in a glass tooltip, clicking pins it open (the only way on a touchscreen), and Escape or a click elsewhere closes it; screen readers hear the text as the button's description, so headers take one line.

On phones both columns dissolve into a single stack ordered by what needs a person first: the questions card, the decision card, late work, the line, next actions, the goal map, agents, activity. Wide tables keep their first column pinned while the figures scroll beside it inside the card. Popovers and the workspace switcher open as a glass sheet across the top of the screen, over the header. The timetable puts each task's label above its track. The tab rail scrolls sideways and fades at its right edge to say so.

## Elevation & Depth

Two layers, as in Apple's Liquid Glass: content and the functional layer above it. The content layer is flat, and its depth is tonal: white panels on the lavender ground, filled wells and hover fills, the shaded future past the now line. The functional layer is glass: translucent, blurred and saturated, with a hairline rim, so it reads as floating over the content rather than as another band of colour.

### Glass Vocabulary

- **Bar** (`glass-bar`: `glass` fill, `backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(1.6)`, an inset hairline at the bottom in `glass-rim`): the sticky header. The fill sits on a `::before` so the header is not a backdrop root and the switcher inside it can blur the page. Below it, a 1rem scroll edge fades the content (`glass-fade` to transparent) instead of a hard line.
- **Float** (`glass-float`: `glass-thick` fill, `blur(28px) saturate(1.6)`, an inset rim, an inset top sheen in `glass-sheen`, and the float shadow): the workspace switcher, popovers and the phone sheet. Thicker, because these hold text and forms.
- **Fallbacks**: without `backdrop-filter`, under `prefers-reduced-transparency`, or under `prefers-contrast: more`, glass becomes solid `panel`; under increased contrast the rim becomes `edge`, and the scroll edge is removed.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Float** (`box-shadow: 0 18px 40px -16px oklch(22% 0.06 292 / 0.3), 0 3px 10px -3px oklch(22% 0.06 292 / 0.14)`; dark `0 18px 40px -16px oklch(8% 0.02 292 / 0.6)`): glass menus and popovers, the only things that float, and the cumulative flow's tooltip.
- **Raised** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px oklch(22% 0.04 295 / 0.14), 0 1px 1px -0.5px oklch(22% 0.04 295 / 0.08)`; none in dark): the selected segment of a segmented control.
- **Lift** (`--shadow-lift`: a long soft drop under a hairline ring; dark a deeper drop and a faint light ring): the marketing site's product frames only, the pictures of the app that float over the page. Nothing in the app lifts.

### Named Rules

**The Functional Layer Rule.** Glass is for navigation and controls that float above the content: the header, the switcher, popovers and the phone sheet. Cards, lanes, tables and charts are never glass, and nothing that is content sits on glass.

**The Flat Content Rule.** Cards, task cards and tables never cast a shadow, and a card never sits inside another card. If something needs to stand out, give it a state tone, not elevation.

## Shapes

Controls are capsules: buttons, tabs, badges, count chips, segmented controls and the workspace switcher are fully rounded. Containers are rounded rectangles that grow with their size: 4px for chart bars, chips and the Now flag, 10px for inputs, task cards, menu rows and code blocks, 14px for cards, 16px for menus, popovers and sheets. Round shapes also belong to the line: the hollow dot where a track sets out, the green dot where it arrives, the stations on the goal map, and the boulder. Each board column is headed by a 4px capsule rail in its station colour, and its task cards stand on the ground beneath it rather than in a box; a dropped track ends in a short slash.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** capsules, 36px tall; small buttons are 32px with a hit area extended to 44px.
- **Primary:** violet with white text, darker violet on hover. One or two per view (see The Prominence Rule).
- **Tinted:** a violet wash with violet text, for the forward step repeated on each row (Done).
- **Secondary:** a filled grey capsule, a shade deeper on hover.
- **Ghost:** text only, for the secondary actions in a row (Release, Drop, Open board, Mark answered), the account menu's trigger and its Sign out, and every manual entry point (Add manually, Add task, Add sub-goal).
- **Danger:** crimson text on a crimson wash, and it always asks for confirmation.
- **Menu rows:** a full-width ghost row inside a `⋯` menu (`Button` size `menu`), icon first, for the rare moves of a task (Release, Drop) or a request (Answered in the conversation). The `⋯` trigger is a ghost capsule that is always visible and is named "More actions for …".

**The Undo or Confirm Rule.** Frequent, reversible actions run at once and offer Undo: Drop, Done, Reopen, lane moves and answers say what they did in the status region ("Dropped “Read the thread”" · Undo), and Undo runs the reverse transition that already exists. Rare actions with consequences ask first: Revoke, Forget, Abandon, Remove, Mark answered, and Mark achieved while tasks are still open. If everything asked, people would learn to click through.
- **Signal:** ember with dark text, used at most once per dusk band.
- **Dusk:** quiet text controls on a dusk band, lifting to `dusk-2` on hover.
- **Focus:** a 2px violet outline offset 2px; on a dusk band the ring turns ember.

**The Agents First Rule.** Agents fill the ledger, so adding a task or goal by hand is always a ghost button, never primary or secondary, and never a form laid open on the page. It sits in the page header or a card header and opens a panel. Connecting an agent is what a new workspace is asked to do first.

### Chips

- **Style:** badges are 20px capsules, a tint of their tone with the tone's ink. Every status, priority, role and memory kind renders through its `{ label, tone }` table in `src/lib/domain.ts`.
- **Counts:** the tab rail's counts for what needs a person (Overview, Decisions) are ember capsules with dark numerals; the Tasks tab's inbox count is a neutral capsule (`fill`, `ink-2`), so ember keeps its meaning.
- **Filters:** the memory kinds are capsules, violet-tinted when selected and filled grey otherwise, each with its count.
- **Lane counts:** a neutral capsule beside the lane's name; a folded lane reads "5 of 13".
- **Who acted:** an agent is a squared tile with a bot, a person a round disc, both neutral; a task's holder is meta text, "Held by Finance clerk", never a violet badge, since violet is a state.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner Style:** 14px.
- **Background:** panel; the question and decision cards are ember and the late card crimson, in both their border and header, and a tinted header's description takes the tone's ink rather than grey.
- **Shadow Strategy:** none (see Elevation & Depth).
- **Border:** one hairline.
- **Internal Padding:** 20px; the header is ruled off from the body.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** panel, a 1px edge border, 10px corners, 36px tall, full width of their wrapper (size the wrapper, not the control).
- **Focus:** the border turns violet and a soft 3px violet halo grows around it, as on Apple's text fields.
- **Error / Disabled:** errors show inline under the form in late ink; disabled controls go filled with muted text.

### Segmented controls

A grey capsule well with capsule segments; the selected one is raised (white with the raised shadow in light, a lifted violet-grey in dark). Used for the timeline window (14, 30 or 90 days), the MCP client tabs in Connect an agent, the task or goal choice in manual entry, and the Light, Dark and System choice of the appearance picker, whose segments carry a sun, a moon and a display.

### Navigation

The glass header holds the boulder wordmark, a `/`, the workspace switcher (a capsule that opens a glass menu), the search box (from 768px; phones use the Search tab), and the account menu: a ghost capsule with the person's initial on a violet disc and their name (the initial alone on phones), which opens a glass popover with their name and email, the appearance picker, and Sign out. Below them, the tab rail (Overview, Decisions, Tasks, Goals, Timeline, Memory, Documents, Search, Agents, Members, from `WORKSPACE_PAGES` in `lib/workspace-pages.ts`) is a row of capsules: muted until hovered, which fills them grey; the active tab is a violet-tinted capsule with semibold violet text. Inline text links underline on hover and thicken the underline while pressed (the `link` utility).

### Menus, popovers and sheets

Glass floats with a 16px corner that grow in from their anchor (220ms, from 97% scale and 4px up). A panel takes focus when it opens (its first field, or itself), opens upward when there is no room below its trigger, and gives focus back to the trigger when Escape closes it; an open trigger keeps its fill. A form panel has a small heading ("Edit task") and a ghost Cancel beside its primary button, closes itself on success and reports in the status region. On phones panels become a sheet at the bottom of the screen, 12px from each edge, so what they act on stays in view above them, and scroll inside themselves when taller than the screen; the answer to an agent's question opens inline under the question instead.

### Status region

One polite `role="status"` region per workspace, a glass float at the bottom centre of the screen. Actions say what they did there, naming the object, with Undo when the reverse exists; a notice stays about six seconds, longer while it is hovered or holds focus, and Undo is reachable with Tab. The background refresh speaks there too, only when something new waits on the person, and at most once a minute.

### Tasks layouts

The Tasks page draws the same tasks four ways, chosen in a segmented control of links with icons (Board, Table, Calendar, Map), each layout its own URL (`?view=`), with the Goal and Status filters (`ToolbarSelect` capsules, through the shared `QueryFilter`) beside it and carried from layout to layout.
- **Table**: a card holding a full-width table, the first column pinned on phones (`PINNED_CELL`). Headings are links that sort (`aria-sort` and a small arrow on the leading one); empty cells read "None" or "No one" in muted text; the due date reads as everywhere else (`InlineDue`, crimson when late).
- **Calendar**: a month as a ruled table, Monday first, days outside the month on a `fill` wash, today ringed in late crimson (the Red Line Rule's now), each task a station-coloured dot and its title, late ones in late ink, three a day before "N more". Phones get a list of the days with tasks instead of the grid.
- **Map**: a left-to-right tree on a card under the station key, goals as panel boxes with the goal map's status marks (and their status in words unless active), tasks as panel boxes with their station dot, titles on up to two lines, "Decision for you" in wait ink and "Late" in late ink, joined by `edge` curves; it scrolls inside its card, a screen tall at most. The boxes are a nested list, so the hierarchy is read aloud, and the curves are decorative; below 640px the same list is an indented outline.

Across the table, calendar and map, a task's station dot names its status for screen readers (`StationDot`, with an `edge` ring so the pale stations hold 3:1), what waits on a person keeps its ember mark, and the task open in the side peek is marked selected (`TaskPeekLink`: `aria-current`, an accent outline or bar).

A status bar's legend (`FlowLegend` with `hrefFor`) turns each status into a link to its tasks as a table, and its segments into the same links for the pointer; the bar then hides itself from assistive tech, since the legend says the same.

### Search suggestions

The header's search and the Search page's box are one combobox (`SearchForm` with `suggestFor`): a pill input, and under it a `glass-float` list grouped under small muted headings (Go to, Show tasks, Goals, Tasks, Documents, Memory), each option a label with muted detail at its right (a page found by another word names the word), the highlighted one on `accent-tint` with an accent bar at its left edge, and a last row, ruled off, that searches for the words. Arrow keys move, Enter opens, Escape closes then clears; the count is announced politely.

### Board stages and Settings

The board's lanes are the workspace's stages, edited on the Settings page (from the workspace menu, never the tab rail, so configuration stays out of the daily view). Each stage takes its kind's station colour on its rail; a hidden lane that still holds tasks says "Hidden". More than four lanes scroll sideways on a wide screen rather than squeeze. Each lane splits into two rows, People above Agents, told apart by structure rather than colour: a caption per row (a person or bot icon, the row's name in sentence case, its count in muted figures), a hairline rule above Agents, and on a wide screen a subgrid that lines both rows up across the lanes so the captions and the rule read as one horizontal band. Rows are never tinted; ember stays for the cards that wait on a person. An empty row is a quiet dashed outline, and a segmented control in the header (People and agents, One row) turns the split off. A narrow card with Resume shows Edit as its pencil alone, so its actions stay on one line. A card's ⋯ menu lists every visible stage under "Move to", so a phone or a narrow lane can still go from Inbox straight to Done. Each lane's header (its rail, name and count) stays pinned as the lane scrolls, so a long People row never leaves the Agents row without its lane's name: under the glass bar on the page, or at the top of the board itself where more than four lanes make the board its own scroller, a screen tall, on wide screens.

### Documents

A document reads like a page of the ledger: its title as the page's headline (Markdown headings sit a level below it), Markdown set in the body face with token colours, tables ruled in hairlines, code on `fill`, and Mermaid diagrams drawn on a panel in the appearance's own theme, with their source one click away. On a task, each document is a quiet `fill` chip with a document icon and no arrow, since it stays inside Phean. The editor is a monospace field with a live preview beside it on wide screens and a Write and Preview switch on phones.

### Peeks

A task or a document opens in a peek over the page it was opened from, never in place of it. A task opens as a **side peek** at the right edge, so the board or list stays in view beside it (the way Notion's side peek opens database items, and because Jira's users pushed back when an overlay hid the list they were triaging). A document opens as a **centre peek**, wide enough to read, with **Full screen** to fill the window for long reading or editing. Every peek keeps the item's own URL: Back closes it, a refresh or a shared link opens the item's full page, and **Open as page** goes there on purpose. A peek is a native modal dialog: the page behind is inert under a light ink scrim, focus moves into the peek and returns to what opened it, and Escape closes it. Its toolbar is a glass bar; its content sits on the ground, never on glass. On phones a peek is the whole screen.

### Selection

What is selected must never be a guess. The selected request on Decisions and the overview (the one holding focus, or the one J and K moved to) carries an accent bar at its left edge, an accent tint, and `aria-current`; a board card whose peek is open takes an accent outline and tint. Colour is never the only sign, and the selected item is scrolled into view as the selection moves.

Sources for these choices: Linear's [peek](https://linear.app/docs/peek.md); Notion's [open pages as side peek, centre peek or full page](https://www.notion.com/releases/2022-07-20); feedback on Jira's [side panel](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-Service-Management/Jira-Cloud-filter-results-now-open-issues-in-a-side-panel-can/qaa-p/3241087); the W3C [dialog (modal) pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialogmodal/).

### Keyboard shortcuts

`?` opens a legend of every shortcut as a glass sheet (it is also in the account menu). Keys are shown as small `kbd` capsules in a hairline outline. Shortcuts never fire while focus is in a field.

### Toolbar filters and search

A toolbar filter is a capsule at the height of the segmented control beside it, "Goal: All goals ▾", with a label for screen readers (`ToolbarSelect`); fields inside forms stay rectangular. A search box above a list is a pill with a clear button, and `/` focuses it from anywhere on the page (or opens Search). Matched words in results are marked with a violet wash (`accent-tint`), never the browser's yellow.

A popover's trigger is a small button, except where the panel leads elsewhere rather than acts: **Resume** uses the link trigger (`Popover look="link"`), violet semibold text with its icon, underlined on hover, with the same 44px hit area as a small button. It sits at the end of a task card's action row, after the state actions, and never wraps onto a line of its own in a board lane. Its panel lists each agent's session: the agent, its app and when it linked, the brief, a tinted **Resume in …** link, and the ids and command as monospace values, cut to one line, each with a ghost copy button named for what it copies ("Copy session id").

### Outside links

Everything that leaves Phean goes through `OutsideLink` and ends with an up-right arrow icon, the one arrow a control may carry. Web links open in a new tab, with `rel="noopener noreferrer"` and "(opens in a new tab)" for screen readers; app links (`cursor://`, `slack://`) open their app in place. On the overview, the asking agent's **Resume in Claude** is the entry's primary button, because answering in the agent's own conversation is the quickest way through; a task's source label ("From email") becomes a quiet outside link when the agent recorded a web link to the message.

### Logo

The mark is drawn in `src/components/logo.tsx` from one `MARK` geometry on a 32-unit square: a concave hill rising from the bottom left to the top right edge, and a boulder (a circle of radius 6.4 at 12.65, 19.17) resting on the slope partway up. The boulder is ember, lit from the upper left (`signal`) with a crescent of shade on its downhill side (`signal-shade`). The wordmark sets "Phean" beside it in 700 at title tracking. The favicon (`src/app/icon.svg`) and the Apple touch icon (`src/app/apple-icon.tsx`) put the same mark on a dusk tile, a gradient from `dark-dusk-2` (`#3b296b`) to `icon-tile-bottom` (`#211541`), with the dark-appearance hill and boulder colours and the slope carried past the tile's edges. The favicon adds a faint white rim (30% opacity, 1.2 units) so the tile keeps its square on dark tab strips, where the dusk ground would otherwise melt into the chrome. Inside a workspace, `components/favicon-badge.tsx` redraws the favicon so the boulder carries a count, the way mail apps show unread mail: a larger boulder in the icon's own ember, lit from the upper left, drawn where the original rests and covering it, so there is one boulder, with the rim drawn again over it, with dark figures counting decisions, approvals and inbox tasks (the tab rail's Decisions and Board counts). Past 99 it stretches into a pebble reading "99+", and at zero the plain mark returns. It is drawn into the page's own icon links, because browsers pick icons by size and type, so an added PNG would lose to the SVG.

### Marketing site (signature)

The landing (`app/page.tsx`, `components/marketing/`) is the one Persuade surface, so it is louder than the app while speaking its language. The message is the product's: **task management, flipped**. Agents capture, track and finish the work, people make the calls, and a fleet of agents needs one place to ask. The name is explained once, in its own section: Phean is short for *Sisyphean*, and the boulder that rolls back every morning is now the agents' to push. Personal assistants are named as the agents Phean helps most.

- **World:** light first, as Stripe and monday.com are. A `brand-mesh` (radial fields of violet, ember, sky and green from the tokens, drifting slowly, at under half strength in dark so they read as colour rather than glow) sits behind the hero and the closing call to action. Two dusk bands carry the name and the decisions, the page's committed colour fields. A turning conic glow (`data-glow`, the brand hues blurred, fainter in dark) lights the hero's picture of the product from behind.

The hero's second sentence is solid `accent-ink`; there is no gradient text, no eyebrow or kicker above a heading, and no arrow glued to a button label. Ledes are `text-lg` in `ink-2`. Calls to action use `Button` size `lg`.

- **Motion:** one 14-second loop shared by every `data-demo` (a request arrives, the cursor picks, the toast says it was sent, the agent hears, the card crosses to Done), the hill's 13-second loop (`data-hill`: the crew climbs, holds while the call is made, and fades as the next boulder waits; at rest it shows the call made), the marquee of agent names (`data-marquee`, wrapped and still under reduced motion), the hero's glow, `data-rise` scroll reveals, and the mesh drift, a transform on an oversized `::before`, never a repaint. ### Docs site

`/docs` is a Read surface in the ledger's own system: the glass bar with the wordmark and "/ Docs", the sections down the left (the current page in `accent-tint` with an accent bar and `aria-current`), a 72ch reading column at `text-md`, and "On this page" beside it from 1280px. ### Flow bar

Open work by station as one stacked bar in flow order, with a legend of swatches and counts. It names itself for screen readers ("Open work by status: 5 inbox, 4 to do, 5 in progress").

### Timetable (signature)

Every task is a track on one time axis, grouped under its goal (or flat, on the overview). Inbox and to do are dashed in their station colour, in progress is a solid violet bar, arrival is a green dot, a drop is a slash, a due date is a short ink tick, and a late stretch is shaded crimson up to the red now line. The Now chip sits on the date baseline and any date label it would cover is left off. Hovering a track dims the others. The window shows the last 14, 30 or 90 days plus a week ahead, and starts with the history when the history is shorter.

### Cumulative flow

A stacked area of how many tasks stood at each station at the end of each day: done is the floor, the open stations ride on it, and a widening band is work piling up. "Show the numbers" opens the same data as a table. With fewer than three days of work it says the chart fills in instead of drawing a sliver.

### Goal map

The goal tree as a line with stations: a violet ring per active goal, a dashed ember ring while proposed, a filled green dot when achieved, a grey one when abandoned, branches for sub-goals, and a flow bar of each goal's tasks with its percentage done.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** use the token names (`bg-panel`, `text-ink-2`, `border-rule`, `bg-accent`, `text-on-accent`, `bg-station-progress`) and nothing from Tailwind's default palette.
- **Do** check a new colour pair in both appearances: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for lines and controls.
- **Do** use `glass-bar` or `glass-float` for anything in the functional layer, so the fallbacks for reduced transparency and increased contrast come with it.
- **Do** give every chart a text equivalent: a `role="img"` name built with `describeCounts`, track notes in words, or a "Show the numbers" table.
- **Do** use the `link` utility for inline text links so they have hover and pressed states, and the `figure` utility for stat values.
- **Do** pin the first column of a wide table with `PINNED_CELL` from `src/components/ui/table.ts`, and make its scroller `relative`.
- **Do** put motion behind `motion-safe:` and keep it brisk: 120ms for colour changes, 220ms for menus and the timetable's dimming.
- **Do** route manual task and goal entry through `ManualEntry` (page headers) or a ghost `Popover` (card headers).
- **Do** add `data-*` hooks (`data-task`, `data-goal`, `data-segment`, `data-end`, `data-now`, `data-tick`) for UI tests instead of selecting by class.

### Don't:

- **Don't** set small labels in uppercase or letter-spaced; use sentence case at the label size.
- **Don't** put more than two filled violet buttons in a view, or ember anywhere but the boulder, the counts, the inbox and the dusk band's call to action.
- **Don't** make a card, board column, table or chart out of glass, or put glass inside a card.
- **Don't** use red for anything that is not late, now, urgent or destructive.
- **Don't** add shadows to cards, or gradients beyond the dusk band's sky and the ground's two faint top glows.
- **Don't** use a weight above 700 or a font other than the system face and Inter.
- **Don't** glue arrows to button text or put an eyebrow line above a heading. The up-right icon on an outside link is not decoration; it says the link leaves Phean.
- **Don't** clamp text over another element; shorten it (task cards cut descriptions at 80 characters and agent questions at 90, with a three-line clamp only as a guard) or move it.
